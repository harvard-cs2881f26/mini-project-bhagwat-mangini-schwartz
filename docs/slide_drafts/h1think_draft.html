The question for H1 is whether small models can close the gap to large ones, and thinking is the cheapest lever I tried. Verdict: partly. Thinking closes most of the gap for the smallest models, but not for free, and the large model gains nothing from it. Text benchmark, 319 items, one run per setting, accuracy is the mean of the 4 exact-letter accuracies, cost is API dollars per 319 items. Nano goes from 49% to 66% at default thinking and 68% at high. Mini goes from 67% to 72% and 73%. GPT-5 stays flat (75%, 73%, 73%) while cost goes from $1.24 to $3.68 to $6.64. Mini at default is within 1 point of GPT-5 at default for about a fifth of the cost. Flash-Lite at high (77%, $1.05) is level with GPT-5 at min (75%, $1.24). Caveats: differences under about 5 points are within noise at n = 319 with one run per setting. Flash-Lite at default barely thinks, so its jump is really min to high. Claude and Gemini Pro were not run at high and are left off. Source: results/index.csv (runs repro_fig4, repro_fig4_default, repro_fig4_high).
