Injury: does more thinking help?

Some thinking = big gain for small models. More thinking = much higher cost, little extra gain

High thinking helps Gemini Flash-Lite and nobody else. I added a high setting next to min and default for the 5 models I ran it on (Opus 4.5, Sonnet 4.5 and Gemini 3.1 Pro are shown at min and default only, they were not run at high). From default to high the GPT-5 family barely moves: average accuracy Nano 66% to 68%, Mini 72% to 73%, GPT-5 73% to 73%. None of these changes is significant (every Holm p is .9 or higher) and about 5 points is noise at n = 319 with one run per setting. It is still more expensive. A full run costs $0.81 vs $0.31 for Nano, $1.52 vs $0.72 for Mini and $6.64 vs $3.68 for GPT-5, and output tokens per item go from 2.3k to 6.2k, 1.0k to 2.2k and 1.0k to 1.9k. GPT-5 at minimum thinking is still ahead of default and high on severity after the action (66% vs 58% and 59%). Flash-Lite is the exception, +6.6 points on risk type (86% to 93%, Holm p below .001) and +5.6 on action effect (69% to 75%, p = .024). Its default setting barely thinks (286 output tokens per item, same as min), so this is really min to high. Gemini 3 Flash moves 1 to 2 points. Source: results/txt0_repro_fig4, txt1_repro_fig4_default and txt40_repro_fig4_high.
