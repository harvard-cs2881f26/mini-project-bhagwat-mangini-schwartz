This is our reproduction of the paper's Fig 4 on the text benchmark: 319 scenarios, the paper's prompt verbatim, thinking at the minimum each provider allows, one run. Paper values are read off its plots since the authors published no exact numbers. Only the 3 GPT-5 models are the paper's exact models. The Claude and Gemini rows (in results/README.md) are newer stand-ins. Risk type reproduces, and every model does worse on the action questions than the scenario-only ones, as the paper says. Two things differ. First, severity. The paper's Fig 3 defines severity as 4-choice accuracy, but its plotted values fit 'share of truly severe cases caught' better (mean gap 0.07 vs 0.17 for exact level). That metric only counts misses (false negatives) and ignores over-flagging (false positives), so always answering severe would score 100%. We kept exact level and report the mismatch as a finding. Second, small models. With thinking at minimum GPT-5 Nano is the outlier (55.5% on risk type), and with default thinking it reaches about 87%, which is the paper's number. So 'small models lag' is partly 'small models at minimal thinking lag'. The default-thinking run (txt1, same prompt and items) is the third table: GPT-5 Nano reaches 86.8% on risk type, the paper's 87%, so the size gap at minimum thinking is mostly a thinking-setting effect. It does not carry over to the action questions, where Nano stays at 63% and 55% against 64% and 66% for GPT-5 at minimum thinking. Source: results/txt0_repro_fig4, results/txt1_repro_fig4_default.
