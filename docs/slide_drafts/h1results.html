H1 is whether decision models (safety classifiers) can spot physical risks at lower cost and within the latency a robot needs. Verdict on text: close, not better. Jev at its best setting (full wording, 4 cumulative questions) scores 73.2% against 75.2% for GPT-5, at 0.70 s vs 3.29 s per item and $0.038 vs $1.24 per 319 items. It beats GPT-5 Mini (66.9%) and Nano. Not every decision model gets there: Kev-4B scores 55.6%. None of them beats GPT-5. The fair rival is a small LLM: Flash-Lite scores 70.4% at 1.47 s, also inside the 2.5 s window, for $0.25. Setup mattered most for Jev: 58.1% with the short wording and independent questions, 73.2% with full wording and cumulative questions (the latter makes 4 calls, so latency and cost are higher). Caveat: each decision model is shown at its best cell from a grid, picked after seeing all 319 items, so it is exploratory until the cross-validated analysis. The chart shows 4 of the 6 decision models. LLMs are at thinking min (txt0). The 2.5 s line is the time to act used on the team's video slide. Source: results/index.csv, docs/part2_writeup.md.
