Injury: thinking min vs. default

Thinking = big gain for GPT-5 Nano (56% → 87% on risk type). Large models barely move

Thinking matters for the small GPT-5 models and almost nowhere else. GPT-5 Nano goes from 56% to 87% on risk type, which is the paper's number, and gains 16 points on action effect and 15 on severity after the action. GPT-5 Mini gains 11 points on action effect. The large models barely move: Gemini changes by about 1 point on average and GPT-5 gets slightly worse on severity (77% to 72% on Q2, 66% to 58% on Q4), though I have not tested whether that is more than noise at n = 319. Reasoning helps most on the action questions, not on spotting the risk. It is also expensive. Nano at default thinking uses 2,271 output tokens per item against 366 at minimum (6.2x), and still trails GPT-5 at minimum thinking on severity (59% vs 77% on Q2, 55% vs 66% on Q4), where GPT-5 uses 220 tokens. Two caveats. Claude gave identical answers and token counts in both runs, so it did not think in either one and says nothing about reasoning. For Gemini the recorded output tokens barely change while latency roughly doubles, so I cannot tell how much it thought. Source: results/txt0_repro_fig4 and results/txt1_repro_fig4_default (per_item.csv).
