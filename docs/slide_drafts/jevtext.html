Jev on the text benchmark, 319 items, 5 settings (results/index.csv: txt4, txt9, txt10, txt11, txt15). Accuracy is the exact letter per question, parse failures are 0.0% in all five. The baseline is the short prompt wording with each question asked on its own (58.1% mean). Letters instead of option names barely moves it (59.5%). Asking the 4 questions cumulatively, so later questions see earlier answers, gives 64.1%, and the full prompt wording gives 65.9%. Both together give 73.2%, which is GPT-5's 75.2% minus 2 points at about 1/30 the cost ($0.038 vs $1.24 per 319 items) and 0.70 s vs 3.29 s per item. The gain is mostly on Q2 to Q4 (severity and the action questions), and Q3 at 74.9% is above GPT-5 (64.3%). Jev's cost is per token, so the cumulative setting costs more because it makes 4 calls ($0.038 vs $0.014). Caveat: the best cell was picked after seeing all 319 items, so it is exploratory until the cross-validated analysis confirms it. Source: docs/part2_writeup.md and results/part2_designs.
