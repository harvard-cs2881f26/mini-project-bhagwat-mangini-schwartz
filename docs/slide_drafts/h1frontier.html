H1 asks whether small, cheap safety models can do the job of a big LLM. Verdict on text: a small classifier plus GPT-5 gets level with GPT-5 for less than half the price, but we have not shown it is better. Every number is the share of questions answered correctly, averaged over 4 questions on 319 scenarios. GPT-5 alone gets 75% for $3.90 per 1,000 items and 3.3 s per item. The small LLM, Flash-Lite, gets 70% for $0.78 and 1.5 s. The classifier, Jev, gets 73% for $0.12 and 0.7 s, about 1/30 of GPT-5's cost, but it is 2 points below GPT-5 and I have not tested whether that gap is real. The last row is a mix: Jev answers first and GPT-5 is called only on the items Jev is least sure about, about half of them. It gets 76% (range 73% to 79%) for $1.69, which is 43% of GPT-5's price. It is fast when Jev is sure (0.7 s) and slower when GPT-5 is called, about 4 s for those items, so it is not faster than GPT-5 on the hard half. How the mix was found: a search tried 60,078 combinations of classifiers and LLMs. It picked on 4/5 of the scenarios and scored on the other 1/5, repeated over 25 splits, so the mix number is on scenarios it never saw. The other rows are each model at its best setting, picked on all scenarios, so they are a little optimistic. Caveats: one benchmark, one run each, and the search is exploratory.
