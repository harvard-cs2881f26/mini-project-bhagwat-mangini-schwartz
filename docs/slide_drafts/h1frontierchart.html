H1 asks whether small, cheap models can do the job of a big one. Verdict on text: a small classifier plus GPT-5 gets level with GPT-5 for less than half the price, but we have not shown it is better. Both charts show the share of questions answered correctly (higher is better), averaged over 4 questions on 319 scenarios. Left chart, cost. GPT-5 alone gets 75.2% for $3.90 per 1,000 items. The blue dots are the best mixes of a small classifier with a bigger model, scored on scenarios the search never saw. For $0.07 a mix gets 72.8%, for $1.69 it gets 75.9%, and for $3.10 it gets 78.0%. At the same cost the big models used alone do worse: GPT-5 Mini gets 66.9% and Flash-Lite gets 70.4%. The classifier Jev alone gets 73.2% for $0.12, about 1/30 of GPT-5's cost, but it is 2 points below GPT-5 and I have not tested whether that gap is real. Right chart, speed. The mixes reach about 77% with a median time under 1 second. The median hides the slow half: those mixes send about half the scenarios to GPT-5, and those take about 4 seconds. The mixes on this chart also cost $2 to $3.70 per 1,000 items, close to GPT-5 itself, so the speed is paid for with money. How the mixes were found: a search tried 60,078 combinations of classifiers and LLMs, picked on 4/5 of the scenarios and scored on the other 1/5, repeated 25 times. The other dots are each model at its best setting, picked on all scenarios, so they are a little optimistic. Flash-Lite with extra thinking scores 76.6% but takes 9 seconds and costs $3.29, so it is not a cheap rival. Caveats: the ranges around the blue dots overlap GPT-5, so read this as level, not better. One benchmark, one run each, and the search is exploratory. Source: results/router_search/ and results/index.csv.
